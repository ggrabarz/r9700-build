    Platforms and lanes¶

    Four 32 GB cards need four slots, 128 GB of VRAM, and enough PCIe to keep them fed. The registry says the cards are rarely the bottleneck; the platform is. This page collects what the corpus learned about boards, lanes and the three interconnect traps that decide whether TP=4 runs at all.

    The board classes that appear¶

    Threadripper PRO / WRX90 WRX90 WS EVO (B1), WRX90E-SAGE SE (B24, B50, B52), MC62-G40 (B22) 128 lanes, 4× Gen5 x16 The throughput tier. Most expensive

    Lane width: what actually costs you¶

    The corpus has measured comparisons at every width, and the ordering is not intuitive.

    Link Build Result
    Gen4 x16 ×4 B3, B18, B44 The reference. 623 tok/s @16 (B3)
    Gen5 x16 ×4 B14, B50 236 → 1,192 tok/s (B14); the only Gen5-specific gain in the file
    Gen5 x4 B20 "Little performance loss even at x4" — x4 is tolerable
    Gen4 x8 / Gen3 x8 B9, B34 80–90 t/s on a 27B FP8 @100k; 84 t/s c1, 250–300 @C8
    Gen3 x16 ×4 B45 137.6–153.6 t/s flat to 251k — older Gen at full width is fine
    Gen3 x4 converter, no P2P B49 Flash-Next TP4 117.89 t/s mean; DS4.1 20.15 — a floor, not a platform verdict
    Gen3 x1 B41 316 PP / 50 t/s vs 5,187 / 118 at Gen4 x16 — 16× prefill loss

    The rule: width matters more than generation, and x1 is a cliff. Four cards at Gen3 x16 (B45) beat four cards at Gen4 x8 for real serving; four cards at Gen3 x1 (B41) are not a serving machine. A single-lane riser is the one link the corpus says never to use.

    The three interconnect traps¶

    1. P2P is a kernel, BIOS and BAR question — not just "atomics on"¶

    • ACS off in BIOS is the first step. B14's owner measured 19.7 GB/s vs 11.6 GB/s busbw with ACS disabled.
    • HSA_AMD_P2P=Y must be in the kernel build. On a two-card Supermicro H13SAE-MF, amd-smi topology reported every peer ENABLED while rocm-bandwidth-test returned N/A, until a kernel with HSA_AMD_P2P=Y was built — then 28.1 GB/s unidirectional / 55.5 GB/s bidirectional. Kernels from 7.1.3 (Fedora 44) enable it by default; check grep HSA_AMD_P2P /boot/config-$(uname -r) before blaming ACS.
    • BAR0 above 2^44 silently breaks it. B33: two of four cards had BAR0 above 16 TiB, so amdgpu_device_is_peer_accessible() rejected those destinations, KFD recorded peer access in one direction only, and every denied peer copy exited 139 instead of returning an error. No amount of ACS or IOMMU tuning fixes placement.
    • In a VM the BAR ceiling is fixable: cpu: host,guest-phys-bits=44 in the Proxmox config moved the BAR below the limit and /sys/module/amdgpu/parameters/pcie_p2p flipped to Y (L1T 254183). Pass through the GPU functions, not the whole device, or the guest reports atomics unsupported.

    2. A PLX switch does not automatically bypass the root complex¶

    B34 put four cards behind a PLX88096 on an AM4 board, all trained Gen4 x16. Peer traffic still traversed the root complex: 14.25 GB/s unidirectional / 27.77 GB/s bidirectional, an effective ~32 GB/s cluster cap against 63 GB/s theoretical. amd-smi topology showed every peer ENABLED the whole time — the topology table is not proof of switch-local routing.

    The fix is a per-switch ACS override (acs-plx-switch-p2p.sh from the Radiance tree), which on bare metal bought +500 tok/s prefill on Flash-Next and brought B38's TRX40 box "on par with SP3 / WRX80 / Xeon quad rigs at PCIe 4 x16". It did not work inside QEMU.

    Also from B34's board: an AM4 board refused to enumerate four cards behind the switch until Legacy CSM was disabled, and the onboard 10 GbE disappeared while BAR space was exhausted (pci=realloc=on present throughout). Budget for Above 4G Decoding + ReBAR + CSM off before buying the fourth card.

    3. Consumer boards have count-specific failure modes¶

    • B11's B850 board runs four cards at x8/x4/x8/x4 and documents a P2P hang.
    • B49's Z790 + i9-12900K with a PCIe converter at Gen3 x4 has no P2P, and the ladder is the interesting part:
    Cards Gemma4-31B Q4_K_M
    1 25.2 t/s
    2 (tensor split) 18.8–19.4 t/s — slower
    4 (TP4) 39.8 t/s

    On a no-P2P host, the second card can make things slower because the reduction crosses the host. Upgrade 1 → 4, not 1 → 2 → 4. The same box also showed GPU_MAX_HW_QUEUES=1 worth 2.7× (20.15 vs ~6.1 t/s at =4), even though =4 shortened the isolated all-reduce tail 5× — microbenchmark wins do not transfer on a DMI-limited host.

    NCCL_PROTO=Simple Every TP≥2 recipe RCCL tuning-index bug on gfx1201 deadlocks otherwise

    HSA_ENABLE_IPC_MODE_LEGACY=0 vLLM-ROCm ≥0.21 recipes Avoids hipIpcGetMemHandle: invalid argument
    amdgpu.ras_enable=0 B34's kernel args, one report 40 → 97 t/s in that report
    iommu=pt B14, B23, B33 Used when cards show DMA instead of iommu-group identity; B18 runs stock IOMMU and works, so it is conditional, not universal

    3 p2p_links per KFD node B14 boot receipt Radiance refuses --p2p on if peer links are missing
    Milan quadrant mapping / NPS=1 B14 note 4 IOD quadrants mean cross-quadrant P2P goes over Infinity Fabric

    Slot arithmetic¶

    Four dual-slot cards need 8 expansion slots, and "many of today's processors, including big Threadripper boards, have only seven" — the fourth card's frame must carry an eighth slot past the board edge. This is why B39 uses a SilverStone SEDA H2 and why Wendell's answer on a 4U RM44 was "5/6U — you CAN do it with the 4U but you'd need the thicc 15k arctic fans".

    Buying rules distilled¶

    1. EPYC Rome/Milan with 64 CPU-direct lanes is the cheapest path to four x16 cards (B3 at $10,733.91; B21 at $7,035 on consumer X570).
    2. Verify P2P before buying the fourth card: HSA_AMD_P2P in the kernel, ACS off, BAR0 below 2^44, and a rocm-bandwidth-test number — not just amd-smi topology.
    3. Do not put a quad behind a PLX switch unless you can run the ACS override on bare metal. In a VM, expect root-complex routing.
    4. Never use x1 risers; x4 is tolerable, x8–x16 is normal.
    5. AM5 works but has a hard ceiling: x8/x4/x8/x4, P2P hangs, and the 1 → 4 jump.
    6. Server chassis are power-limited, not lane-limited — see power and cooling.
