    R9700 SOTA Handbook¶

    State of the art as of 2026-11-07, distilled from 62 completed research sessions in this T3 project (63 including the consolidation session that produced this handbook). The raw transcripts are preserved in session-archive/ at the project root — this site contains only the findings that survived verification.

    The subject is a 4× or 8× AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700 (32 GB, gfx1201) LLM inference box, built for performance per dollar rather than peak benchmark scores.

    The answer in one page¶

    Question Current answer
    Best default build The proven EPYC 7452 quad — 4 × ASRock R9700 Creator at 210 W, ROMED8-2T/BCM, 128 GB DDR4-2666, one 1 TB NVMe, vLLM-Radiance at 2 × TP=2, Qwen3.8-27B FP8 + FP8 KV + MTP. $10,733.91/node.
    Measured anchor 623 tok/s at 16 users, 837 W; 493 written tok/s across 12 cards (41.1 tok/s/GPU) on real jobs; $18.15/Mtok; break-even 3.4 h/week vs GPT-5.6 Sol.
    Throughput ceiling Radiance TP4 monolith on TR PRO 9955WX / WRX90: 236 tok/s single-stream, 1,192 tok/s at 16 concurrent, 11.27 ms/step. Roughly 3× the same box on llama.cpp.
    8 cards in one chassis? No — build two quads. The octo is +17 % $/GPU before chassis/UPS, only wins on GLM-5.3-Flash TP=8 (+29.6 %/GPU), and cannot serve the proven Qwen3.8-27B at TP=8 (refused by name).
    Best model on this silicon Qwen3.8-27B FP8 for dense serving; Qwen3.8-Flash-Next for the flagship MoE; GLM-5.3-Flash for best perf-per-dollar MoE.
    Engine family vLLM-Radiance / standalone Radiance (libr4d, 25 hand-written RDNA4 kernels), not upstream vLLM. Strata for layer-split + expert streaming.
    Hard limits No FP6/FP4 on RDNA4; decode is DRAM-bound below M ≈ 300 rows/GEMM; no NVLink/xGMI; no validated 8-card air build.
    Do not build 8 GPUs on one 128-lane socket for Qwen-27B; 6 cards; 8 × 16 GB with Strata; 8 × 256 GB RAID0; BF16 gpt-oss-120b; DeepSeek V4.1 FP8 through cpu_offload.

    What "SOTA" means in this handbook¶

    Every claim carries one of these tags:

    Tag Meaning
    MEASURED A published run on R9700 hardware, with the model/quant/context named.
    REPORTED The builder's own claim without an independent run; usable, but labelled.
    DERIVED Arithmetic on measured inputs (cost per token, residency, lane counts).
    VERIFIED Checked against a primary artefact (vendor manual, spec sheet, source file).
    UNKNOWN No source found. Never silently upgraded to a fact.

    Anything that failed verification, was retracted, or was superseded is moved to Evidence & corrections rather than deleted — so the same mistake is not re-made by a later session.

    The three builds¶

    1. Default — the proven quad¶

    4 × ASRock Radeon AI PRO R9700 Creator 32 GB   @ 210 W each
AMD EPYC 7452 (Rome, 32C, 64 PCIe 4.0 lanes)  ~$127 used
ASRock Rack ROMED8-2T/BCM                      AST2600 BMC
8 × 16 GB DDR4-2666 ECC RDIMM = 128 GB         170.6 GB/s
1 × Samsung 990 PRO 1 TB NVMe
SilverStone HELA 2050R / RM44 4U
vLLM-Radiance, 2 engines × TP=2
Qwen3.8-27B FP8 + FP8 KV + MTP, ≤262,144 ctx
$10,733.91 per node

    This is the only configuration with published end-to-end numbers on this exact silicon. It deliberately avoids the 128-lane wall instead of solving it.

    2. Throughput — the Radiance TP4 monolith¶

    Threadripper PRO 9955WX + WRX90, four cards on CPU-direct PCIe 5.0 x16, 122–128 GB DDR5, Radiance standalone TP4 with ar_oneshot_4rank + --tp-wire exact. 236 tok/s single / 1,192 tok/s at 16 — about $18k–$25k, roughly double the spend for 2–3× the peak. Worse $/token, better latency.

    3. Strata — the capacity box¶

    Same quad, but 256 GB RAM (8 × 32 GB) and 2 × 2 TB or 1 × 4 TB Gen5 NVMe. Strata does layer split + expert streaming, not tensor parallel. This is the box that runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and the DeepSeek V4.1-Flash ISTA packs without falling into --mmap-experts low-RAM mode.

    Full reasoning: Verdict.

    The four facts that decide everything¶

    1. The bandwidth/compute crossover is M ≈ 300 rows/GEMM. Decode is far below it, so decode is DRAM-bound and no kernel choice rescues it. The levers are resident-expert hit rate, bit-width, working-set placement, and a cheap dequant→dot path.
    2. RDNA4 has FP16 / BF16 / FP8 / INT8 — and no FP6/FP4. A model shipped in FP4 must be dequantised on the fly. MXFP4 is reachable as a Quark container or a W4A8 fp8-WMMA path, not as a native tensor op.
    3. There is no NVLink or xGMI. Every GPU↔GPU byte crosses PCIe. Many small TP groups beat one large one: DP × TP=2, never one TP=8 group on a PCIe-only box.
    4. Host RAM is the expert arena, NVMe is capacity, VRAM is compute. A bigger GPU does not lower the RAM a Strata model needs. Size the RAM tier to the expert arena, not to a round number.
