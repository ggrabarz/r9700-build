    Cost & TCO¶

    The proven quad is $10,733.91 and costs $18.15 per million output tokens on real jobs. That is the only fully measured cost anchor on this part. Everything else in this chapter is either arithmetic on it or a different platform measured the same way.

    Line Per node Note
    4 × ASRock Radeon AI PRO R9700 Creator 32 GB $7,244.58 $1,811.15 each — shortage-adjusted street
    AMD EPYC 7452, 32 cores $127.17 Rome, 64 PCIe 4.0 lanes
    ASRock Rack ROMED8-2T/BCM $1,162.39 AST2600 BMC included
    8 × 16 GB DDR4-2666 ECC RDIMM (128 GB, 8 ch) $860.59 170.6 GB/s — not $7,600 of DDR5
    Samsung 990 PRO 1 TB NVMe $254.39 one drive

    SilverStone HELA 2050R Platinum $578.92 2050 W
    ARCTIC Freezer 4U-M Rev.2 $56.96
    Fans (3 × P12 Pro + 2 × NF-A8) $49.57
    Carrier frame + paste + alcohol $10.95
    Total $10,733.91 three nodes = $32,201.72

    Eight GPUs as two nodes: $21,467.82 — cheaper than the single-octo estimates ($23.9–28.5 k), because a quad on Rome needs no PCIe switch, no bifurcation and no 128-lane part.

    The ratio that reframes the exercise: the four cards are two-thirds of the cost. The rest of the machine is cheap by comparison. Puget's version of the same fact: "The AI capability is the pair of R9700s … adds over $3,000."

    2.3 All-in, with hardware amortised¶

    On an $18,775 workstation amortised over 3 years, 780 W total, 156.2 tok/s:

    Duty cycle $/Mtok
    168 h/week (full-time) $1.46
    8 h/week $26.94

    Moving from 8 h/week to full-time serving cuts token cost 18×. Moving the power rate from $0.10 to $0.25/kWh moves it 0.8–15 %. Your duty cycle decides this, not your power rate.

    API Break-even

    3.4 hours per week against the most expensive API is the headline. If the box runs more than that, it is cheaper than GPT-5.6 Sol.

    3. The three levers that actually move tokens-per-dollar¶

    1. Use the Radiance stack and turn MTP on. Worth 2.06× at c=8 over stock vLLM, and MTP another 1.61× — free, because MTP validation is exact and the output is bit-identical. On the same two cards: 15.88 → 62.75 tok/s at c=1.
    2. Buy the cards, not the workstation. Priced as a pure GPU upgrade (cards ≈ $3,283 of an $18,775 machine), break-even against GPT-5.6 Sol falls from 7.2 h/week to 1.3 h/week.
    3. Do not buy enterprise NVMe, and do not buy 512 GB of RAM. Consumer Gen5 is 2.5–4.4× cheaper per TB for the same sequential bandwidth, and the reference node needs one drive. The 256 → 512 GB RAM jump is +$14,800 — more than the entire GPU pool at MSRP.

    4. The octo is worse per dollar¶

    Fact Number
    Incumbent (two quads) $21,467.82, $2,683/GPU, 41.1 tok/s/GPU, $18.15/Mtok
    Cheapest orderable octo $3,147/GPU — +17 % before chassis/UPS
    Vendor liquid octo $7,250/GPU
    GLM-5.3-Flash at TP=8 53.25 tok/s/GPU, +29.6 % — break-even BOM $27,807
    Qwen3.8-27B on the octo (4 pairs) $22.14/Mtok vs $18.15 — 1.22× worse
    Production profile 8 × 262,144 needs 33.0 GiB/rank against a 9.1 GiB budget

    The octo's only exclusive workload is GLM-5.3-Flash at TP=8, and the gain does not cover the platform cost until the BOM is below $27,807. The proven model cannot use the octo's headline feature at all. Price the octo against $27,807, not the vendor's $58,004.

    5. Cross-platform: R9700 vs V100 SXM2 vs CMP 170HX¶

    All three as 8-card machines, measured or derived as tagged:

    R9700 octo V100 SXM2 octo CMP 170HX octo
    Complete machine $28,900 $9,000 $37,600
    3-year TCO $37,876 $19,045 $48,771
    $/GB VRAM all-in $112.89 $35.16 $73.44
    GLM-5.3-Flash 426 tok/s 53.0 tok/s not viable
    DeepSeek-V4-Flash ~58 tok/s 73.5 tok/s not viable
    Qwen3.5-122B-A10B — 329 tok/s aggregate —
    Cost per GLM token 1.0× 4.4× worse —
    Native FP8 / INT4 yes no no
    Coolable 8-card build none yes (4029GP-TVRT) mining frame only

    The two facts that decide it:

    1. The V100 is half the price and 8× slower on the model in question. $19,045 TCO vs $37,876, but 53 vs 426 tok/s on GLM-5.3-Flash — 4.4× more per GLM token. Cheap to buy, expensive to run.
    2. The V100 is competitive exactly where FP8 matters least. On DeepSeek-V4-Flash it does 73.5 vs ~58, and it does 329 tok/s on Qwen3.5-122B. It loses on FP8-designed MoE because it must dequantise NVFP4 into FP16 on 2017 tensor cores.

    The CMP 170HX is excluded on kernel support alone: ships as 8 GB, 64 GB needs a Linux-only unlock, FP32 locked ~1/8, FP64 disabled, no vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT, PCIe 2.0, no warranty — and it is the most expensive of the three, with prices up 1,097 % in 90 days.

    The mixed-traffic flip condition: if DeepSeek grows past roughly a third of tokens, the V100's 4.4× GLM penalty starts to wash out against a 50 % cheaper machine. That is the condition to watch, not the spec sheet.

    7. Cost answer in one line¶

    At $10,733.91, 623 tok/s at 16 users, 837 W, $18.15/Mtok and 3.4 h/week break-even, the proven quad is the cheapest measured way to own this throughput. Spend the next dollar on RAM (256 GB) and one 4 TB Gen5 drive if the box runs Strata — not on a bigger chassis, a second octo, or 512 GB of memory.
