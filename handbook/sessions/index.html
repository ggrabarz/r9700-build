    Session ledger¶

    63 T3 threads ran in this project between 2026-10-22 and 2026-11-07: 2,453 messages, 659 assistant turns, 39 threads that produced output, and 24 that died before their first token.

    Every row has a full transcript in session-archive/threads/, with the mapping in session-archive/index.json. The archive is the raw record; this page is the index to what each session actually contributed to the current state of the art.

    Reading a zero-output thread

    A thread with 0 assistant messages is a model route that died — a guardrail block, a silent gateway death, or account-wide budget exhaustion — not a session that chose to do nothing. The output file may still exist because a later route or the supervisor wrote it. research/AGENTS.md records the route history.

    The program in six waves¶

    Wave Dates Threads What it settled
    1 — Eight-agent survey 22 Oct 9 The R9700 silicon facts, the engine survey, the ROCm/kernel stack, the five model architectures, and the first storage/topology design. Five of eight routes died; the files were completed by later routes.
    2 — The build-guide delta 22 Oct 9 A real 93-page, three-unit quad-R9700 build guide arrived. All eight agents re-derived their slices against it. It overturned nine wave-1 conclusions, including the octo, the 300 W assumption, iommu=pt, and "Strata is the only engine".
    3 — The octo verdict 23–24 Oct 10 Eight slices answered "8 cards in one chassis?" 0 unconditional yes, 1 no, 7 yes-if — each conditional on a different model than the one the proven quad serves. The answer became two quads.
    4 — Three platforms 24 Oct 28 R9700 vs V100 SXM2 vs CMP 170HX, on price, TCO, power, kernels and feasibility. Five of six agents died on budget; the late V100 return corrected the estimate by 18×.
    5 — The real-build registry 30 Oct – 7 Nov 6 The Strata sizing review and a 31-round internet sweep that produced 52 verified 4-card builds, B1–B52, plus the per-entry evidence in sources/.
    6 — Consolidation 7 Nov 1 This handbook, the session archive, and the retirement of the other 62 sessions.

    What each wave changed in one line¶

    1. Wave 1 got the silicon right — 640 GB/s, 64 CU, 191/383 TF, gfx1201 — and the model shapes right, but assumed an octo and missed the production engine.
    2. Wave 2 replaced the octo with a quad, 300 W with 210 W, DDR5 with DDR4-2666, the multi-SSD array with one 1 TB drive, and "Strata only" with vLLM-Radiance.
    3. Wave 3 proved the octo is a single-model machine (GLM-5.3-Flash TP=8), not a platform upgrade: +17 % $/GPU, a 20.9 % sync tax, no air-cooling path, and Qwen3.8-27B refused at TP=8 by name.
    4. Wave 4 found that the cheapest 256 GB of fabric-connected memory is a used Supermicro 4029GP-TVRT at $8,500, but it is 2017 silicon with no FP8/FP4 — so it wins on DeepSeek and dense Qwen and loses 4.4× on GLM.
    5. Wave 5 replaced estimates with 52 real builds: the $10,733.91 proven quad, the $7,035 cheapest quad, the 1,192 tok/s Radiance monolith, and the corrections that came from owners (B3's full body, B49's TP4, the retracted djdeniro figure).
    6. Wave 6 distilled all of it into the pages you are reading and archived the raw transcripts.

    What the 24 empty threads prove¶

    They are the reproducibility record. The project's most expensive failure mode was not a wrong fact; it was a model route that reads "ready" and produces nothing. The rule that came out of it — probe every candidate with a one-word prompt before committing a brief — is in research/AGENTS.md and is the reason later waves used opencode/* zen routes.
