    Hardware¶

    The R9700 is a RDNA 4 workstation card, not a datacenter part. AMD's own footnote says the Radeon PRO W6000/W7000 and Radeon AI PRO R9000 series are "not designed nor recommended for datacenter usage". The design that follows treats it as a lab / small-office GPU server: one node, no RDMA, a BMC for out-of-band recovery, and a power budget that fits a normal circuit.

    1. The silicon¶

    Property Value
    Architecture RDNA 4, gfx1201
    Compute 64 Compute Units, 128 AI accelerators, 4,096 stream processors
    Memory 32 GB GDDR6, 256-bit, 640 GB/s, 64 MB Infinity Cache
    Matrix paths FP16 191 TFLOPS · FP8 383 TFLOPS · INT8 383 TOPS · INT4 766 TOPS (published)
    Missing paths FP6 and FP4 do not exist on RDNA4 — they are CDNA-only
    TBP 300 W default; 210 W is the operating cap used here
    Host link PCIe 5.0 x16 (Gen4 x16 in practice on the proven Rome platform)
    Fabric none — no NVLink, no xGMI; GPU↔GPU is PCIe only

    The consequence that governs every model decision: the usable quantisation lattice is FP16 / BF16 / FP8 / INT8. A model whose weights ship in FP4 must be dequantised on the fly. MXFP4 is reachable as a Quark container or a W4A8 fp8-WMMA path, not as a native tensor operation.

    The crossover that decides decode¶

    The bandwidth/compute crossover sits at M ≈ 300 rows per GEMM for every precision (FP16 298, FP8/INT8 299, INT4 299), because peak scales with bit-width while DRAM does not. Decode is far below 300 rows, so decode is bandwidth-bound and no instruction choice rescues it.

    The only levers are:

    1. resident-expert hit rate (VRAM vs RAM vs NVMe),
    2. bit-width (fewer bytes = more experts resident),
    3. keeping the working set in VRAM,
    4. a cheap dequant→dot path.

    Measured on the R9700, the expert-cache read hit 637–640 GB/s — the full DRAM ceiling — which proves the point: the GPU is already maxed on memory.

    2. The platform decision: quad, not octo¶

    2.1 The lane wall¶

    8 GPUs × x16 Gen5 = 128 lanes on paper, but the 8-slot SP5 boards do not give 8 × x16. The ASRock Rack manuals (GENOAD8X-2T/BCM p.8/p.18, TURIND8X-2T/500W p.8) show:

    128 lanes
├── 7 × PCIe 5.0 x16   = 112 lanes
├── 1 × PCIe 5.0 x8    =   8 lanes
├── 2 × NVMe x4        =   8 lanes
└── total              = 128 lanes, no PCIe switch in the GPU tree

    The 8th GPU rides x8; SLOT1 drops to x8 if MCIO4/M2_2 are populated; SLOT7 shares lanes with MCIO1/2. So the wall is real, but its shape is: you get 8 GPUs and 2 NVMe at full width on a switchless board, and every further NVMe is bought by demoting a GPU link. The storage tier — not the GPU tier — is what needs a switch.

    Platform Add-in Gen5 lanes 8 × x16 feasible?
    EPYC 9005 (SP5), 1P (9655P / 9555P / 9455P / 9355P) 128 Yes — 7 × x16 + 1 × x8 on TURIND8X
    EPYC 9005, 2P up to 160/socket Yes, over-capacity / over-cost
    Threadripper PRO 7965WX / WRX90 128 Gen5 CPU lanes; ~144 usable platform total Yes, with bifurcation — WRX90E has 7 × x16; the 8th GPU takes one slot split into 2 × x8
    Threadripper TRX50 48 Gen5 CPU lanes (80 on TR 9000), 88 usable No

    A 128-lane single socket leaves zero lanes for NVMe and the NIC after 8 × x16. The proven answer is to not build the octo: a quad needs 4 × x16 = 64 lanes, which Rome provides exactly, and the second quad is a second node.

    Decision: build two proven quads. Reconsider the octo only if a >128 GB working set becomes a material share of traffic, and price it against $27,807, not the vendor's $58,004.

    2.4 Memory sizing¶

    Both viable boards wire 8 DIMM slots 1DPC at DDR5-6400 → ~410 GB/s board bandwidth. EPYC silicon is natively 12-channel (614.4 GB/s/socket); the EEB board exposes 8 of 12 channels to free PCIe real estate.

    Why 8 channels is not a checkbox: Strata-GLM-AMD measured GLM-5.3-Flash decode at 30–39 tok/s on two cards and found it bound by host RAM bandwidth — "a machine with faster memory channels writes faster." With 8 GPUs and a larger expert arena, the RAM tier is on the critical path.

    Price risk (October 2026 street): 8 × 32 GB DDR5-5600 RDIMM ≈ $950 each = $7,600; 8 × 64 GB DDR5-6400 RDIMM ≈ $2,799.99 each = $22,400. The jump from 256 GB to 512 GB is +$14,800 — more than the entire GPU pool at MSRP. Re-quote at procurement; memory is moving weekly.

    Size the RAM tier to the expert arena the model actually needs, not to a round number.

    3. Power and cooling¶

    3.1 The 210 W cap¶

    • The cards accept 210–300 W; the cap lives at power1_cap under /sys/class/drm/card*/device/hwmon/, in microwatts.
    • It reverts to 300 W after a cold power-off, so a service must set it at every boot.
    • At 210 W every card sat at its power limit almost all the time under load, with junctions averaging 81–90 °C (92 °C peak) — the limit sets the speed, not the temperature.
    • Capping 4 cards to 210 W doubled gpt-oss-120b TP4 decode (44.6 → 89.4 tok/s) in one measured build. A dual-card BetterBench check found 151.5 tok/s at 210 W vs 153.0 at 300 W — +50 % card power buys ~1 % decode.
    • Uncapped, one build hard-powered-off ~7 minutes into warm-up.

    3.2 Circuit and PSU¶

    Configuration GPU power System wall Amps @120 V
    4 × R9700 @ 210 W 840 W ~1.1–1.3 kW ~9–11 A
    4 × R9700 @ 300 W 1,200 W ~1.5 kW ~12.5 A
    8 × R9700 @ 210 W 1,680 W 1.93 kW 16.1 A
    8 × R9700 @ 300 W 2,400 W 2.65 kW 22.1 A → 240 V
    • 4 cards at 300 W already sit near a 120 V / 15 A branch limit; 4 × R9700 (128 GB) is the practical maximum per box on a normal circuit.
    • Proven PSUs: Thermaltake GF3 1650 W, Lian Li 1000/1200 SFX, Corsair HX1200i, Seasonic 2200 W, ASUS PRO WS 2200 W, Seasonic Prime PX-2200.
    • Fails: BeQuiet Dark Power 14 1200 W (TP load oscillation), Hydro PTM PRO ATX3.1 1650 W (not enough 8-pin connectors).
    • Radiance's custom all-reduce creates multi-kHz in-phase load steps that trip some ATX 3.x PSUs. The Dark Power 14 1200 W fails; the GF3 1650 W, Lian Li 1000/1200 SFX and Corsair HX1200i are proven.
    • The card vendor changes the connector count: PowerColor ships a 2 × 6-pin → 12VHPWR adapter; the others need 3 feeds. Four PowerColor cards therefore need 8 PCIe feeds against 12 for other vendors.
    • An OEM 8-GPU server rated at 300 W/card (Gigabyte G291-Z20) crashed four R9700s on transient spikes; the 210 W cap did not fix it, and the fix was one card per dual bay. Treat an OEM 8-GPU chassis as a 4-card box unless you add an external supply.

    3.3 Cooling¶

    • Four dual-slot cards need 8 expansion slots; many Threadripper boards have only seven. The frame must carry an eighth slot past the board edge (e.g. SilverStone SEDA H2).
    • Stock BMC fan curve follows the CPU. With four cards working, the CPU stays cool, the fans stay slow, and the card area hits its 68 °C warning and the run PAUSES.
    • The proven fix is a software fan service: hold Card Side Temp at 62 °C, 30 % duty at 200 W total GPU power, 55 % at 840 W, full at 65 °C or an unreadable sensor, backstop 90 °C → 30 %, 95 °C → 100 %, and always hand the fans back to the BMC on stop. ipmitool raw 0x3a 0xd6 <duty> sets duty; 0x3a 0xd8 0|1 switches manual/automatic — ROMED8-2T/BCM only.
    • 1-slot waterblocks exist (Alphacool, ~€320/€325 per block) for ASRock Creator, ASUS TURBO, PowerColor and Sapphire — not Gigabyte. They are for >4 cards; a 4-card box that fits with airflow is lower-risk on air (a failed loop kills far more than a failed heatsink).
    • A second uncapped 300 W quad (four ASRock cards, cramped spacing) held 69–70 °C at 100 % on air under Radiance — so "one card per dual bay" is a chassis rule, not a universal thermal law.

    4. Remote administration¶

    The requirement is a BMC with an out-of-band path. Concretely: an ASPEED AST2600 on the server board, giving IPMI-over-LAN, KVM-over-IP, Redfish, remote power cycle and BIOS access — all independent of the host OS.

    Both candidate boards carry one (Supermicro AS-4125GS-TNRT; Gigabyte G494-SB0-AAP2, whose manual's validated-GPU list explicitly names the Radeon AI PRO R9700S). The proven ROMED8-2T/BCM carries an AST2600 too.

    5. Platform gotchas that are hardware, not software¶

    • Bare metal, not KVM/VFIO. ncclCommInitRank hangs under passthrough even with full 27.6 GB/s P2P. Bare metal initialises in 0.12 s. Never rebind vfio-pci ↔ amdgpu at runtime — it can panic the kernel.
    • IOMMU stays in translated mode. AMD's docs suggest iommu=pt to prevent hangs, but on gfx1201 peer reads return 0xFF garbage and peer writes are silently dropped with it. Silent corruption is worse than a hang.
    • BAR space is a 4-card problem on consumer boards. An AM4 board refused to enumerate four cards behind a switch until Legacy CSM was disabled; the onboard 10 GbE disappeared while BAR space was exhausted. Budget for Above 4G Decoding + ReBAR + CSM-off before buying the fourth card.
    • The 2^44 BAR ceiling is fixable inside a VM: cpu: host,guest-phys-bits=44 in the Proxmox config moves the BAR below the DMA limit and pcie_p2p flips to Y. Pass through only the GPU functions or PCIe atomics report unsupported in the guest.
    • A PLX switch does not automatically bypass the root complex. Four cards trained Gen4 x16 still traversed the root complex at 14.25 GB/s unidirectional / 27.77 GB/s bidirectional; amd-smi topology showed every peer ENABLED the whole time. Verify with TransferBench, and apply per-switch ACS overrides.
    • P2P needs HSA_AMD_P2P in the kernel, not just BIOS/atomics. On a two-card H13SAE-MF box every peer read ENABLED while rocm-bandwidth-test returned N/A until a kernel was built with HSA_AMD_P2P = Y; afterwards 28.1 GB/s unidirectional / 55.5 GB/s bidirectional.
    • On an SP3/Milan board the slot link may have to be pinned to Gen4 before TP=4 will run — one EPYC 7663 owner had to hardcode Gen4 instead of Auto to make the onboard Gen5 switch behave.

    6. The hardware answer, one line¶

    A 4-card Rome quad is the smallest machine that is known to work, and two of them are cheaper and faster than one 8-card SP5 box for the workload this project actually serves. The 8-card machine is a different product for a different model; it is not an upgrade.
