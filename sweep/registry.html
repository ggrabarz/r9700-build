    What the host classes buy¶

    The single biggest price lever is not the cards; it is the platform. Four classes recur.

    EPYC Rome/Milan (SP3) — the value tier¶

    B3, B4, B6, B18, B28, B32, B42, B44, B45 (and B36's Huananzhi Rome board)

    Rome's 64 CPU-direct PCIe 4.0 lanes are exactly enough for four x16 cards, which is why the cheapest complete quads cluster here: $10,733.91 for B3's EPYC 7452 / ROMED8-2T box, and $7,035 for B21's consumer X570 build with the same four cards. The tier's weakness is single-thread and memory bandwidth, not lanes: DDR4-2666/3200 boards still deliver 623 tok/s @ 837 W (B3) and 137–154 t/s flat to 251k context (B45).

    Threadripper and TR PRO — the throughput tier¶

    B1, B2, B7, B10, B14, B22, B23, B24, B25, B27, B38, B39, B50, B52

    TR PRO 9955WX builds hold the file's highest single-box numbers (B14: 236 → 1,192 tok/s, 1→16 streams) and the highest prices (B2: $18k; B25: €17,414.88 as a component set). WRX90 also buys Gen5 x16 to all four cards without a switch, 8-channel DDR5 and enough BAR space to avoid the consumer-board traps. Nuance: B1's owner lost ~20% from the perf-level=HIGH advice that helped B50, so "TR PRO = free win" is not a rule.

    Consumer boards (AM4, AM5, LGA1700) — the cheap route, with cliffs¶

    B11, B21, B34, B49

    A 9700X/B850 board runs four cards at x8/x4/x8/x4 (B11) and works; a X570 Godlike runs four at $7,035 total (B21). But this is where the failures concentrate: the P2P hang (B11), the PLX switch that does not bypass the root complex (B34), the BAR-space / CSM problem (B34), and the Gen3 x4 converter with no P2P (B49). B49's ladder is the one to memorise: 1 card 25.2 t/s → 2 cards 18.8–19.4 (slower!) → 4 cards 39.8. On a no-P2P host the upgrade path is 1 → 4, not 1 → 2 → 4.

    Server chassis and OEM systems¶

    B32, B37, B41, B46, B51, B52

    Server quads are cheap to buy and hard to power: B37's Gigabyte G291-Z20 is rated 300 W per card and four cards crash it, with the 210 W cap not helping; the fix was one card per dual bay. B46 is the counter-example — four ASRock cards in an EPYC 7742 server at ~300 W each, 69–70 °C, on two 1600 W supplies and two circuits. B51/B52 are commercial quads with no benchmark; they establish the shipping shape (4U, 2500–3000 W, liquid or 8 slots), not performance.

    Some entries are in the registry because they corrected a previous belief. These are the ones worth reading in full:

    B44 Found a throughput cap introduced by an image tag (29.06.19 onward), not by hardware

    B49 The only 1 → 2 → 4 ladder on one host, and the "second card can be slower" result

    What is deliberately not a build¶

    • Dual-card and triple-card machines: dozens of them, many excellent (L1T 251544's 28.1 GB/s P2P pair, lcz.me 2539's Radiance-vs-Strata harness). They inform platforms and engines but never the registry count.
    • Plans with BOMs: lcz.me topic/1936 (¥63,320, unassembled), Anarchaotic's CAD build, Agent9's four-card Threadripper. All in open leads.
    • Fakes: one documented fabrication, in corrections.
