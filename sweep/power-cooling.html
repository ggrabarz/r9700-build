    Power and cooling¶

    The R9700 is a 300 W-class card that behaves like a 400 W card in transients and is happiest at 210 W. Every production quad in the registry runs a cap, and the corpus treats that as the single highest-value free change.

    The 210 W rule¶

    Evidence Result
    B19 — 4× ASRock, gpt-oss-120b TP4 44.6 → 89.4 t/s decode after capping to 210 W
    Dual-card check, 210 W vs 300 W 151.5 vs 153.0 t/s — +50% card power buys ~1%
    B1, B3, B27, B45 All run 210–225 W per card in production
    Uncapped Hard power-off ~7 minutes into warm-up in one report
    Minimum settable 210 W; 200 W is not offered

    The cap is not only thermal. It also keeps a four-card box inside a normal circuit: B27's owner capped at 210 W so total draw stayed ~1,400 W and "plugs into any normal 15 A outlet".

    Transients are the catch: the cards spike to ~400 W despite the cap, and a custom one-shot all-reduce creates multi-kHz in-phase load steps that trip some ATX 3.x supplies.

    Supplies¶

    Proven Fails
    Thermaltake GF3 1650 W BeQuiet Dark Power 14 1200 W — TP load oscillation
    Lian Li 1000 / 1200 SFX Hydro PTM PRO ATX3.1 1650 W — not enough 8-pin connectors
    Corsair HX1200i 850 W — insufficient for even 2 cards in one report
    Seasonic 2200 W / Prime PX-2200
    ASUS PRO WS 2200 W

    Connector arithmetic depends on the card vendor. PowerColor ships a 2× 6-pin → 12VHPWR adapter, while "all others I've seen will consume 3×" — so four PowerColor cards need 8 PCIe feeds versus 12 for other vendors. That is the difference between a 1600 W unit with four 12VHPWR leads and a unit that cannot be cabled at all.

    Two supplies are normal at this size. B46 runs 2× 1600 W Great Wall on two separate circuits; B37's server chassis is limited by its per-card 300 W rating; B41 runs a full 300 W per card on DC server supplies and a breakout board in an open frame and reports it "very stable so far".

    Whole-machine draw¶

    Build Figure
    B39 (media quad) ~123 W idle with all four cards installed; 500–900 W under AI load
    B27 ~1,400 W total at 210 W/card, on a 15 A outlet
    B46 "about 300 W at peak" per card, ~1.2 kW class, two circuits
    B1 ~1,600 W total, 230 V, cards undervolted below 210 W
    B3 837 W while serving 623 tok/s @16

    At 120 V, ~1,200 W of GPU power already sits near a branch limit, which is why the corpus calls four R9700 "the practical maximum per box". On 230 V / 16 A (3.7 kW) a 2200 W supply is comfortable.

    Server chassis are power-limited¶

    The Gigabyte G291-Z20 (B37) is rated 300 W per card, and four R9700s crashed individual cards with spikes above that. The 210 W cap did not fix it; the only fix was one card per dual-card bay. Treat an OEM 8-GPU server as a 4-card box unless you add an external supply. This is the reason B37 stopped at four and why the 8-card question remains a power problem, not a software one.

    B46 is the counter-example that keeps the rule honest: four ASRock cards in an EPYC 7742 server at ~300 W each, 69–70 °C at 100% load on air, on two supplies and two circuits. "One card per dual bay" is a chassis rule, not a universal thermal one.

    Chassis¶

    • Eight expansion slots are required for four dual-slot cards; many Threadripper boards have seven. The frame must carry the eighth past the board edge.
    • B39 uses a SilverStone SEDA H2 for exactly this reason.
    • Wendell on the SilverStone RM44: "5/6U — you CAN do it with the 4U but you'd need the thicc 15k arctic fans."
    • B28's Phanteks Enthoo Pro 2 Server V2 holds the quad with 264 GB DDR4 and a 1600 W supply.
    • B46's Gigabyte MZ01-CE1 server chassis fits four ASRock cards "cramped" and still cools them.

    Water cooling¶

    • A 1-slot R9700 waterblock exists: Alphacool, ~€320–325 per block.
    • Compatibility: ASRock Creator, ASUS TURBO, PowerColor, Sapphire — not Gigabyte (and Alphacool reportedly has no plan for one).
    • The community verdict is that blocks are for >4 cards, not for a quad that fits with airflow. Dense air-cooled quads stay under throttle if exhausts are clear and cold air reaches top and bottom.
    • The risk argument is worth repeating: pumps/fans have a 3.5–6 year 24/7 service life, every fitting is a failure point, and a failed loop is far likelier to kill €5,000 of cards than a failed heatsink. For a 4-card box that fits, air is the lower-risk choice.
    • A German thread confirms multi-card water plans are dying on price, not hardware: one owner bought Gigabyte cards, found the blocks incompatible, and abandoned a 4–7 card plan.

    A pre-flight power checklist¶

    1. Cap every card at 210 W and verify with rocm-smi/amdgpu — not just the driver setting.
    2. Count PCIe feeds per card for your vendor (PowerColor 2, others 3) before choosing a supply.
    3. Budget 1,400–1,600 W for a quad plus host; two supplies or one 2200 W unit.
    4. Prefer two circuits if the box will run 24/7.
    5. Confirm 8 slots and airflow to top and bottom cards.
    6. If it is an OEM server chassis, assume one card per dual bay until proven otherwise.
